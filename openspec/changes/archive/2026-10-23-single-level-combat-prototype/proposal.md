## Why

The project has selected Godot 4 and GDScript, but has no runnable game yet. A compact, playable encounter will exercise the agreed desktop, input, presentation, and gameplay architecture in context, while giving the project an initial visual target and a practical basis for play-testing.

## What Changes

- Add a launchable desktop prototype centered on one bounded, fixed encounter.
- Demonstrate keyboard movement, mouse aiming, primary fire, a destructible hazard, a simple hostile threat, and player survivability.
- Provide clear encounter completion, defeat, and restart feedback.
- Establish original, prototype-grade anime-inspired ship, arena, projectile, and impact visuals without relying on copied or unlicensed artwork.
- Keep this as a technical/gameplay proof: exclude multi-level routes, procedural generation, upgrades, progression, and production-ready content.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `single-level-combat-prototype`: A launchable one-level combat demo used to validate the selected game architecture and establish initial gameplay and visual direction.

### Modified Capabilities
- None.

## Impact

- Adds the initial Godot project scenes, GDScript gameplay components, input configuration, and prototype visual assets.
- Exercises the selected Godot 4/GDScript stack and built-in scene, node, resource, and signal patterns; no new engine, language, or external service is proposed.
- Provides a manual play-test target for keyboard/mouse feel, combat readability, encounter flow, desktop launch, and performance on the project’s M1 baseline.
- Does not alter the broader game’s finite multi-level route, progression, or release scope.
